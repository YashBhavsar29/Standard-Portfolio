01

The Spark

The pitch was simple: the city's electric plant is failing, and you are the repairman sent into its substations to bring the power back. We wanted a calm puzzle where reading the layout is the skill, with real stakes: connect the wrong wire and the substation blows.

We built it for the Genesis 5 game jam, so it had to be small enough to finish and clear enough to understand in a minute.

02

Project Brief

Spark Shift is a 2D cyberpunk puzzle adventure set in a city that has gone half dark. In each substation the player studies a wire layout, fixes the broken wires, and steers the current to the bulb at the end while avoiding distribution boxes that explode.

Core elements

Broken wiresFixed with the wrench; fixed wires carry current ahead.
Distribution boxesThe hazard: current reaching one makes it blast and fails the level.
The bulbThe end of each substation; reach it to clear the level.
Five handcrafted levelsEach substation is more demanding than the last.
ControlsW / A / S / D or arrow keys to move; Enter to fix wires.
SoundCalm background music, with effects for current flow, blasts, terminals, and doors.

The core loop

Enter a substation → Read the wire layout → Fix the right wires → Current flows toward the bulb → Avoid the distribution boxes → Clear the level and move on.

Intended audience

Puzzle players who like atmospheric games, and jam players looking for something quick to understand and quick to retry.

Platform

PC, built in Unity 2D, played with the keyboard and released on itch.io.

01

The core mechanic: routing current

The central idea is that current flows only through wires you have fixed, so the player decides where it goes. The challenge is choosing a path that avoids the dangerous parts of the layout.

  1. Read: The player studies the layout of wires, distribution boxes, and the bulb.
  2. Fix: Pressing Enter near a broken wire repairs it with the wrench.
  3. Flow: Current travels ahead along the repaired wires.
  4. Fail: If current reaches a distribution box, it bursts and the substation is lost.
  5. Clear: If current avoids every hazard and reaches the bulb at the end, the level is cleared.

Because a single wrong repair can fail a level, the game rewards slow, careful looking rather than speed.

02

Building wire lanes from a list of directions

Each level needs long, winding wire lanes. Placing every segment by hand would have been slow, and would have made changing a layout painful in a game jam. So I customized a wire line generation algorithm that builds a lane from a list of directions:

sequence = ["left", "left", "down", "down", ...]

At runtime the generator turns that list into an actual wire lane in the scene, one segment per step. A lane is described as data instead of being drawn by hand, so levels were quick to lay out and quick to rework once we tested them.

03

One script per component

I wrote a script for each part of the puzzle, so each has one job and the rules of a level come from how they interact.

PlayerMoves with WASD or the arrow keys and fixes wires with Enter.
Electric wireBroken or fixed; fixed wires pass current on.
Distribution boxBlasts if current reaches it, ending the level.
BulbThe goal at the end of the lane; lighting it clears the level.
04

Five levels, rising difficulty

The game has five handcrafted levels. Each one asks for a bit more observation and precision than the last, from the first simple lane to layouts where the safe route is easy to miss. I designed them with the wire generator, which let me reshape a layout quickly after playing it.

05

Art and sound

The look is a dark, neon cyberpunk city. The sprite sheets and environment assets were generated with image-generation tools, and I designed how they fit together as the player, the substations, and the environment. The music and sound effects come from online open libraries.

The audio is meant to be relaxing yet immersive: a soothing, rhythmic background track, with effects for current flowing, a distribution box blasting, a power terminal switching on, and a door opening. These effects give the player feedback on every action. The generated art and library audio are third-party, not original work.

06

Retrospective

Spark Shift showed me how much a small tool built early pays off in a game jam. The wire generator took some time up front, but it let me build and rebalance levels much faster than placing segments by hand.

If I revisited it, I would add a check for the direction lists, so a typo or a lane that runs off the layout is caught before the level is played. I would also collect proper playtest data on how long each level takes and where players fail, so the difficulty curve is tuned from evidence rather than feel.

The main takeaway: in a short project, a simple mechanic with clear stakes and a fast way to build content goes further than extra features.
